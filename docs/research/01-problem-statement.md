# 01. Problem statement

## Why pilot stress matters

Commercial aviation is one of the safest forms of transport, and the biggest
remaining contributor to accidents is the human in the loop. Analyses of
accident data over several decades consistently attribute the majority of
accidents, in whole or in part, to human factors such as degraded decision
making, loss of situational awareness, poor crew communication and errors of
attention (Shappell et al., 2007; Boeing statistical summaries).

Stress and fatigue are two of the most common ways a fit, trained pilot
becomes an impaired one. Both degrade exactly the capacities that
flying depends on:

- **Attention** narrows under acute stress ("tunnelling"), so peripheral cues
  such as a warning light or a radio call are missed.
- **Working memory** capacity falls, so checklists are skipped or steps are
  repeated.
- **Decision making** shifts toward faster, more habitual, less analytic
  choices.
- **Communication** deteriorates; stressed crews speak less and challenge
  less.
- **Reaction time and vigilance** decline steadily with sleep loss and time
  awake.

Chronic stress adds a second layer. It disrupts sleep, which compounds
fatigue; it is a leading pathway into depression and anxiety; and it is
frequently hidden, because pilots have strong career incentives not to
disclose it.

## Evidence that this is a live problem

- An anonymous web survey of 1,848 airline pilots found that 12.6% met the
  threshold for depression on the PHQ-9 and 4.1% reported suicidal thoughts in
  the previous two weeks (Wu et al., 2016).
- A systematic review following the Germanwings Flight 9525 accident found
  that depression and suicidal ideation among commercial pilots are
  under-reported, and that fear of losing a medical certificate is a major
  barrier to seeking help (Pasha & Stokes, 2018).
- A study of European pilots reported that most respondents experienced
  work-related stress that affected their well-being, with sleep disruption,
  rostering and job insecurity among the leading causes (Cahill et al., 2021).
- Fatigue has been cited as a contributing factor in numerous accident
  investigations, including Colgan Air 3407 (2009) in the NTSB's findings on
  crew performance, and led directly to the rewrite of United States flight
  and duty time limits in 14 CFR Part 117.

## The gap

Regulators have responded with fatigue rules, fatigue risk management
systems, and (after 2015) requirements for psychological assessment and peer
support programmes. These are operator- and regulator-level interventions.
What they do not give the individual pilot is a simple, private, daily
instrument for noticing their own state before it becomes a safety problem.

The IM SAFE checklist is taught to every pilot and is exactly this kind of
instrument, but it is usually done from memory, unrecorded, and gives no view
over time. A pilot cannot see that they have flagged "fatigue" four times in
the last ten days, or that their perceived stress has been climbing for a
month.

## What this project is

A **simple, private, evidence-based stress monitoring system** that:

1. Takes about two minutes per day and uses instruments pilots already know
   or that are validated in the literature.
2. Produces a **transparent readiness state** (Green, Amber, Red) with the
   reasons spelled out.
3. Shows **trends** over days and weeks so that slow drift is visible.
4. Keeps **all data on the pilot's own device**, so that it cannot be used
   against them and there is no reason not to answer honestly.
5. Points the pilot toward **existing help** (peer support, the aviation
   medical examiner, crisis lines) when the numbers warrant it.

## What this project is not

- Not a medical device and not a diagnostic tool.
- Not a substitute for an operator's fatigue risk management system, a
  regulator's medical certification process, or professional care.
- Not a surveillance system. It is designed so that it cannot be one.

## Success criteria

- A pilot with no training can complete a check-in in under two minutes.
- Every verdict is explainable in plain language.
- No data leaves the device without an explicit export action.
- The scoring rules are documented and traceable to the research in these
  documents.
